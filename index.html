Proposal Context

Relationship pluralization and representative measurement

Representative surveys still capture intimate relationships through a largely mono-normative lens. This module closes that measurement gap and links relationship diversity to inequality, resources and well-being in Switzerland.

Problem Major surveys miss CNM, polyamory and plural relationship forms.
Innovation Piloted items distinguish identity, lived configuration and relationship spectrum.
Payoff First representative Swiss analysis of relationship pluralization and social inequality.
Show background and rationale

Intimate relationships are undergoing profound transformation. While mid-20th century societies converged around monogamous marriage and the nuclear family, recent decades have seen marked pluralization, including consensual non-monogamy (CNM), polyamory, and diverse cohabitation arrangements. Yet representative survey research has failed to keep pace: major instruments continue to measure relationships through a mono-normative lens, recording only legal marital status or single partnerships. As a result, we lack basic population-level estimates of how common non-monogamous arrangements are, who practices them, and under what conditions.

This proposal addresses two interrelated gaps. First, it introduces survey items extensively piloted across three waves with a combined sample of N = 920, with results publicly available at https://drfawint.github.io/love_and_sexuality/, capable of capturing relationship diversity in a representative Swiss sample. The items distinguish between subjective relationship orientation and lived relationship configuration, covering a broad spectrum from monogamy to relationship anarchy. Second, it embeds these measures within a resource-theoretical framework. Existing CNM research treats non-monogamy primarily as an ideological commitment or identity choice, largely ignoring how economic, temporal, and cultural resources may structure both access to and feasibility of non-monogamous arrangements.

Building on the MOSAiCH 2027 / ISSP Social Networks and Social Resources module, which already captures income, education, religiosity, social support, and well-being, our proposed add-on module supplies the missing element: a direct, empirically grounded measure of relationship diversity. This enables a representative analysis of how relationship pluralization in Switzerland intersects with social inequality, normative change and individual well-being.
